# Closet App — LinkedIn post (working draft)

---

Hi LinkedIn, I actually have something to tell you for once. Most of you have no idea that besides the UX work, I'm also a retired blogger. Well, pelamarela is still alive, just not kicking much lately. Anyway.

As it happens, I'd spent the past month buried in Claude for work, poking at agentic design workflows for a client, which means I'd gotten weirdly comfortable telling AI what to do.

I wanted a good way to catalogue my wardrobe and actually use what I own when I get dressed. (*Anyone who lived through Polyvore knows the feeling. RIP, you were too good for this world.*) I tried the apps that promise it. Paywalled, clunky, stuffed with things I didn't ask for. None of them fit.

So one morning, on my way to the office, **I figured I'd just build the thing myself.**

My professional defect kicked in immediately: I planned it first. Wrote a feature list, talked it into a proper PRD. I'd meant to build something purely functional, a DOS-looking thing that just records stuff. But I couldn't help myself, it had to be pretty, so I set up a whole design system and designed every screen before building a thing.

I'd never built anything like this. My developer vocabulary is so thin that "deploy" and "push" meant the same thing to me, and rather than fight it I just let the AI run the whole thing. Accounts, infrastructure, the lot. I mostly nodded along until a screen showed up in the browser.

First version that worked took me about 4 hours. Making it good took 2 more afternoons. That's the trick with these tools, the first 80% is suspiciously easy and the rest is just you, in an endless loop of fixing 1 thing and breaking 5 others.

Which brings me to the point.

It's called Closet. It holds my wardrobe, remembers what I've worn, and helps me decide when I have no clue. My own spec literally said do not add features beyond this scope, and spoiler, I added more within the hour. Cue version 2, where the app can tell me, honestly, whether that thing in my cart is a good idea.

I use it. It's live, it's free, and it's open if you're the kind of person who likes playing in their virtual closet as much as I do.
